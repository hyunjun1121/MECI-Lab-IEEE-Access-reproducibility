\rev{\textit{Anatomical-consistency check.} \mbox{Table~\ref{tab:crosswalk}} maps literature peaks onto the profiles. Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI152) peaks were transformed to the fsaverage (MNI305) coordinate system with FreeSurfer's published affine \mbox{\cite{fischl2012freesurfer}}. The Talairach peak was first converted to MNI152 with the Lancaster transform for coordinates from software other than Statistical Parametric Mapping \mbox{\cite{lancaster2007bias}}. Each peak was then assigned to the nearest vertex of the fsaverage5 mid-thickness surface. A peak is \emph{inside} when both the nearest-vertex surface label and the volumetric Destrieux label belong to the profile, \emph{outside} when neither does, and \emph{uncertain} otherwise. The distribution files of the volumetric atlas disagree on its template space, and a geometric test could not resolve the conflict. Volume labels were therefore read under both MNI152 and MNI305 assumptions, and any disagreement counts as uncertain. Distance is measured from the peak to the nearest profile vertex. Affine mapping with nearest-vertex assignment can be off by several millimeters \mbox{\cite{wu2018accurate}}. Membership and distance describe anatomical consistency only. A peak inside a profile does not validate the profile as a measure of that function, and a peak outside a profile does not show that the territory is functionally unrelated. A peak outside or far from a profile does not change the profile; the corresponding study is cited as functional context rather than as direct support.}

For adjacent overlap-weighted five-second cortical vectors $\mathbf{x}_{j-1}$ and $\mathbf{x}_j$, whole-cortex pattern change is $P_j=1-r_j$, where $r_j$ is their Pearson correlation across the 20,484 corresponding vertices after centering each vector. Mean absolute cortical change is $M_j=\frac{1}{20{,}484}\sum_{v=1}^{20{,}484}|x_{j,v}-x_{j-1,v}|$. These descriptors are calculated from the persisted one-second outputs; the first comparison and any constant-vector pattern correlation are undefined and omitted. The six profile values are not summed into a composite score.

\rev{\textit{Spatial-control analysis (post hoc).} The analysis plan, including the statistic, acceptance rule, test families, and null models, was fixed in a hash-locked document before any rotated region was scored. The plan allowed only bug fixes after locking. Its amendment log records five code changes described as bug fixes or additions that did not alter a rule, an aborted first spin run (stopped by a memory error before any family result was printed; partial files deleted), and one rule change, made 43 minutes after locking and before any literature peak was mapped: the coordinate-transform tolerance, which the plan had not fixed, was set to the rounding bound of the published matrix (0.0033~mm; a discarded first run had used 0.001~mm), and a default MNI152 assignment of the volume atlas was replaced by a rule that leaves the space unresolved unless one hypothesis is better by at least 0.05 on both agreement measures, marking disagreeing labels uncertain. This change affects only the anatomical-consistency check (\mbox{Table~\ref{tab:crosswalk}}), not the spin tests. Candidate rotations were drawn uniformly from the rotation group with seed 33. For a rotation $R$ applied to the left hemisphere, the right hemisphere used the mirrored rotation $FRF$, where $F$ reverses the left--right axis. Each target vertex took the mask value of the source vertex nearest to its inverse-rotated position on the same hemisphere. Rotated vertices on the medial wall were excluded, and rotations retaining fewer than 80\% of the original vertex count were rejected. Every rotated region was scored with the observed profile's statistic on the same cohort. The one-sided $p_{\text{spin}}$ is $(1+\#\{\text{null}\ge\text{observed}\})/(N+1)$. Before any association was computed, each profile was graded on the median vertex ratio, area ratio, spatial spread, and connected-component count of its rotated regions; all six met all four criteria. Rotating a mask forward and back reproduced it with a median Dice coefficient of 0.978--0.995, so nearest-vertex reassignment introduced little distortion. \mbox{Table~\ref{tab:spin}} gives the primary result and \mbox{Table~\ref{tab:spinsens}} the sensitivity families.}

\rev{The conclusion did not depend on the acceptance criterion: with a 50\% criterion or none, the cingulo-parietal profile still exceeded 98.7--98.8\% of rotated regions. Keeping only rotations whose overlap with the original mask had a Dice coefficient of at most 0.2 gave a cingulo-parietal Holm $p$ of 0.053. A parcel-level rotation, which rotates parcel centroids and reassigns parcels one-to-one with the Hungarian algorithm \mbox{\cite{vazquez2019gradients}}, gave 0.047. Size-matched random parcel sets without spatial structure gave nominal $p$ values for the cingulo-parietal (0.021) and anterior-insular (0.047) profiles, with Holm $p\geq0.13$. Adding the cortical mean outside each region as a covariate lowered the cingulo-parietal association to 0.232, which still exceeded 91.1\% of rotated regions ($p=0.089$). This analysis measures relative position after a common cortical component is removed. With three-second windows (186 videos), the observed correlations were smaller than with five-second windows (cingulo-parietal 0.127, anterior-insular 0.163). In that analysis the anterior-insular profile exceeded 96.1\% of rotated regions ($p=0.039$, Holm 0.24). A same-cohort comparison, specified before it was run and not a spin test, restricted the three-second analysis to the 140 videos of the five-second cohort. The three-second correlations were then 0.179 (frontal), 0.229 (anterior-insular), 0.206 (cingulo-parietal), 0.074 (posterior-cingulate), 0.092 (visual), and 0.058 (auditory), against 0.374, 0.417, 0.437, 0.214, 0.209, and 0.081 at five seconds. In the 46 videos eligible only at three seconds, the cingulo-parietal and anterior-insular correlations were $-0.113$ and $-0.037$. The smaller three-second values are therefore not explained by the cohort (\mbox{Section~V-C}). Ten-second windows left only four eligible videos and are not interpreted. For relative retention performance, the cingulo-parietal profile exceeded 99.2\% ($p=0.008$, Holm 0.049). For exit intensity, the observed associations were near zero ($|\rho|\leq0.09$), while the rotated regions were centered below zero. This produced nominal $p$ values for the posterior-cingulate (0.020) and cingulo-parietal (0.033) profiles, with Holm $p\geq0.12$. One persisted prediction file failed its stored checksum and was restored from the archived release; it is not in the five-second cohort, and excluding it from the three-second analysis changed no result. The 72 spin tests are twelve families of six profiles: the primary comparison (\mbox{Table~\ref{tab:spin}}), the nine sensitivity families in \mbox{Table~\ref{tab:spinsens}}, and two families described only in this paragraph, the ten-second analysis and the three-second analysis excluding the restored file. With Holm correction across all 72 tests, the smallest corrected value was 0.57.}

\rev{\textit{Conditional prediction ablation.} Using the window-level Ridge design of \mbox{Section~IV-D}, we added the cingulo-parietal profile, the anterior-insular profile, both, all six, and all six minus one to the metadata model. Video-level MAE was compared with a paired video bootstrap (2,000 resamples). For grouped exit intensity, all six profiles reduced MAE by 0.000220 (95\% interval [0.000071, 0.000374]). The cingulo-parietal, anterior-insular, and paired additions had intervals that included zero. Removing the frontal or the visual profile from the six increased error, with intervals excluding zero; removing any other single profile did not. The chronological split showed a similar six-profile reduction (0.000349, [0.000018, 0.000701]), although its interval included zero when windows were weighted equally (see the window-weighting check above). For audience watch ratio, no profile set reduced error. The incremental information is therefore observed for exit intensity at the level of the six-profile combination, and individual profiles add little. Relative retention performance was not analyzed. Its stored predictions were reproduced with the stored regularization strengths, but the reconstructed selection rule chose different strengths in some folds, so new feature sets could not be fitted under the original rule.}

\rev{\textit{Information overlap among profiles.} Within-video, position-adjusted Spearman correlations between pairs of profiles, averaged over the 140 videos, ranged from 0.19 (cingulo-parietal and auditory) to 0.88 (anterior-insular and cingulo-parietal). With the cortical mean outside both regions added as a covariate, they ranged from $-0.26$ to 0.82, and the anterior-insular and cingulo-parietal pair remained the highest. These values describe information overlap. Different cortical systems may covary under the same audiovisual stimulus, so a high correlation is not evidence against an anatomical or functional distinction.}

\section{Extended Descriptive Analyses}
\label{app:extended}

Extended analyses were completed from the frozen result set but are not promoted to primary claims. Topic summaries describe content-label and outcome distributions; cluster candidates describe retention-curve shapes, not validated audience types. Additional window-width, parcel, API-context, and window-level prediction screens remain exploratory.
